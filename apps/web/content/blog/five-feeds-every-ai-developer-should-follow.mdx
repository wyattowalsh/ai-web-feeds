There are thousands of feeds in the AI space. Most of them are noise — reshares of reshares, hot-take newsletters, and company blogs that publish once a quarter.

Here are five that consistently deliver.

## 1. The Gradient

**`https://thegradient.pub/rss/`**

The Gradient publishes long-form technical writing on machine learning research that sits in the valuable middle ground between Twitter threads and full academic papers. Articles are typically 2,000–5,000 words, written by researchers and practitioners, and cover the "why does this matter" context that papers themselves usually omit.

Particularly strong on: interpretability, evaluation methodology, and field-level retrospectives. If you're building a RAG system that answers questions about ML research directions, this feed is high-priority signal.

**Update frequency**: Weekly to biweekly

---

## 2. Hacker News (Ask HN + Show HN subset)

**`https://hnrss.org/ask` / `https://hnrss.org/show`**

The full HN feed is too noisy for most AI pipelines. The Ask HN and Show HN subsets are much better — they filter for posts where someone is asking a specific question or showing a specific thing they built, which means the text is more structured and the signal-to-noise ratio is higher.

For AI developer tooling specifically, Show HN consistently surfaces projects 6–12 months before they reach mainstream coverage. The comment threads are often more valuable than the posts themselves.

**Update frequency**: ~10–20 items/day combined

---

## 3. Andrej Karpathy's Blog

**`https://karpathy.github.io/feed.xml`**

Karpathy posts infrequently — sometimes months between entries — but each post is a landmark document. "The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Recurrent Neural Networks," "A Recipe for Training Neural Networks," "Software 2.0" — these are pieces that practitioners reference years after publication.

This is a low-frequency, high-value feed. Perfect for a system that needs to track influential long-form thinking rather than daily news.

**Update frequency**: A few times per year

---

## 4. Papers With Code (Latest)

**`https://paperswithcode.com/latest`**

The feed version of Papers With Code gives you the metadata layer on top of arXiv: papers alongside their code repositories, benchmark results, and method classifications. For any system that needs to track the state of the art on specific tasks, this is more useful than the raw arXiv feed because the curation and linking has already been done.

The task and method taxonomy that Papers With Code uses is also a useful reference for thinking about how to organise ML capabilities.

**Update frequency**: Multiple times daily

---

## 5. Simon Willison's Blog

**`https://simonwillison.net/atom/everything/`**

Simon Willison maintains the most consistently useful running commentary on the practical AI tooling space. He writes short, substantive posts about things he's actually built and tested — prompting techniques, LLM quirks, tool integrations — and has been doing so long enough that the archive is a genuine research resource.

The `everything` feed includes link posts and shorter notes in addition to the longer essays, which makes it higher volume but also more comprehensive.

**Update frequency**: Daily to multiple times daily
